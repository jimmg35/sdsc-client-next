## A year of SDSC

Twelve stories reached the SDSC newsroom between **October 12, 2025 and October 12, 2026**, the center's first full year. They begin with the center being founded and end with a sandbox anyone can put their hands in. In between come grants, papers, conference talks and awards, and a lot of the work comes from graduate students as well as faculty.

[anchor:fsu-launches-spatial-data-science-center]
The year opened in October 2025, when Florida State University formally launched the **Spatial Data Science Center** within the College of Social Sciences and Public Policy, with **A. Stewart Fotheringham** as director. The center started with three main projects: **election analytics** across more than **200,000 U.S. precincts**, **explainable GeoAI**, and making **MGWR** fast enough to handle very large datasets on an ordinary laptop. Those three set the direction for much of what followed.

[anchor:interpretability-explainability-geoai-special-issue]
In November, **Ziqi Li** joined guest editors from Peking University, Emory University and the Technical University of Munich in calling for papers for an **ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing** special issue on interpretability and explainability in GeoAI. Submissions were due **May 31, 2026**. The issue follows the center's explainable-GeoAI work and asks the wider field the same question: how do you see why a spatial model gives the answer it does?

## Spring: funding and new work

In April the election work received outside funding. **Mehak Sachdeva**, with co-PIs **A. Stewart Fotheringham** and **Mason Mathews**, received a **2025-2026 FSU/AWS Research Acceleration Fund** award that includes **$20,000 in AWS cloud credits**. The project, _Disentangling Sorting from Socialization_, studies how moving house and neighborhood context shape political outcomes across the United States.

[anchor:mehak-sachdeva-fyap-grant-urban-polarization]
The same week, Sachdeva received a **First Year Assistant Professor Grant** for _Placing Politics_, which is building an individual-level national voter-file dataset covering **2019-2022** to study political engagement and partisan segregation. Together the two awards give the center's political research both the data and the computing power it needs.

[anchor:amber-dejohn-ut-knoxville-digital-health-talk]
**Amber DeJohn** spent the spring taking her aging and mobility research beyond Tallahassee. She was invited to the **University of Tennessee, Knoxville** to speak about digital health in later life.

[anchor:goal-symposium-fsu-2026-wrap-up]
At FSU she hosted the **Geographies of Aging and the Life Course (GOAL) Symposium**, the group's first independent meeting. Researchers in aging, health and geography attended, along with SDSC members including Mason Mathews, Ziqi Li, Weining Kan and Stephen Liwur.

[anchor:amber-dejohn-applied-geography-transit-investment]
She also published a paper in _Applied Geography_, written with Matthew Palm and Matthew Suandi, that estimates how transit investment changes neighborhoods. It does so by comparing them with transit routes that were proposed but never built.

[anchor:sdsc-aag-2026-san-francisco]
The center's largest showing came at the **2026 AAG Annual Meeting in San Francisco** in March, where **3 faculty and 7 graduate students** presented research on mobility, health, local spatial modeling and causal inference. Three members also won honors: **Stephen Liwur** won the HMGSG **Health Data Visualization Award**, **Weining Kan** was elected a graduate student board member of the same group, and **Ziqi Li** was elected to the board of the **Spatial Analysis and Modelling** group. A social event held with FSU Geography brought together more than 25 students, faculty and alumni, along with two former advisees of Fotheringham, **Taylor Oshan** and **Levi Wolf**.

## Summer: papers, travel and recognition

In May, graduate research assistant **Chen-Lun Kao** published _Uncovering Local Spatial Context via Multiscale Geographically Weighted Binomial Regression_ in **Spatial Statistics**, with Sachdeva and Fotheringham. It extends multiscale geographically weighted modeling to outcomes that are yes-or-no, and it builds on work Kao presented at AAG. It shows the center's MGWR work being carried forward by its graduate students.

[anchor:stewart-fotheringham-chongqing-nanjing-2026]
In June, Fotheringham was due to travel to China. He was to give a presentation and receive an **Honorary Professorship** at **Chongqing University**, then deliver a keynote at the **Population Geography Conference** in **Nanjing**. It was further international recognition of his work in spatial analysis, and it took the center's research to audiences abroad.

[anchor:jacob-tagnan-usgif-2026-scholarship]
In August, Ph.D. student **Jacob N. Tagnan**, who works with **Mark W. Horner**, was named a **Doctoral Scholar** in the **United States Geospatial Intelligence Foundation's 2026 scholarship program**. USGIF made **24 awards** this cycle, and only **three** went to doctoral scholars. A national foundation chose the work of an SDSC graduate researcher.

## Fall: something to try

The year closed in October with an **augmented-reality sandbox**, now calibrated and running thanks to **A. Stewart Fotheringham**, **Chen-Lun Kao** and **Jia-Jun Chang**. It reads the surface of the sand as people shape it and projects elevation bands and contour lines back onto it. Hold a hand over the box and it rains, and the water runs downhill into the lowest basin.

The sandbox is not a research instrument, and SDSC does not present it as one. Its value is explanation: a steep slope, a watershed, or the idea that the shape of the ground decides where water goes are all easier to see at the box than on a page. After a year mostly about grants, papers and talks, it gives visitors something to try rather than only something to read about.
