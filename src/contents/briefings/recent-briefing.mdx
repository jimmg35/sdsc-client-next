## The latest from SDSC

Two stories reached the SDSC newsroom between **July 12 and October 12, 2026**, and they could hardly be more different: a national scholarship for one graduate researcher, and a sandbox anyone can put their hands in. What they share is where they start. Both come from the center's graduate research assistants, one recognized for their own research and one setting up something anyone who visits can try.

[anchor:jacob-tagnan-usgif-2026-scholarship]
In August, **Jacob N. Tagnan**, a Ph.D. student in the Department of Geography, was named a **Doctoral Scholar** in the **United States Geospatial Intelligence Foundation's 2026 scholarship program**. USGIF made **24 awards** this cycle, from rising college freshmen to doctoral candidates, and only **three** went to doctoral scholars.

Tagnan works with **Mark W. Horner**, and the award recognizes that doctoral research while tying it to the geospatial intelligence community the foundation convenes. Its weight comes from outside the university: a national foundation, with more than **$2 million** awarded since 2004, singling out the work of an SDSC graduate researcher.

[anchor:sdsc-augmented-reality-sandbox]
In October the center gained something of a different kind: an **augmented-reality sandbox**, now calibrated and running thanks to graduate research assistant **Chen-Lun Kao**. It reads the surface of the sand as people shape it and projects elevation bands and contour lines back onto it. Hold a hand over the box and it rains, and the water runs downhill into the lowest basin.

The sandbox is not a research instrument, and SDSC does not present it as one. Its value is explanation. A steep slope, a watershed, or the idea that the shape of the ground decides where water goes are all easier to see at the box than on a page, and it gives visitors something to try rather than only something to read about.
